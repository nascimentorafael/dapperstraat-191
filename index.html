Getting here

Directions from the airport, the building entrance and the way back.

Apartment address

Finding the entrance

  • The apartment is on the Dapperstraat, the street of the historic Dappermarkt. Google Maps is the easiest way to find your way here.
  • The building entrance is next to Malhi Afro Beauty.
  • The arrow points to the building door. Image: Google Street View

From Schiphol Airport

  • Train: take the train to Amsterdam Muiderpoort station. From the station it is about a 10-minute walk.
  • Taxi: or take an Uber or Bolt straight to Dapperstraat 191.

From Amsterdam Centraal

  • Tram: take tram 14 towards Flevopark and get off at Pontanusstraat. From there it is a short walk to Dapperstraat.
  • Train: take a Sprinter to Amsterdam Muiderpoort (one stop, about 5 minutes). From the station it is about a 10-minute walk.
  • On trams, metros, buses and trains you pay by tapping your bank card or phone on the card reader when you get on and again when you get off.

Coming by car

  • I don’t recommend driving into the city: street parking is expensive, it is charged per hour (weekends included), and free spots are very hard to find.
  • The best option is a P+R (Park and Ride) garage at the edge of the city. The closest ones to the apartment are P+R Zeeburg, in the east, connected to the city by tram 26 and bus 37.
  • The P+R rate is about €6 per 24 hours (€13 for the first 24 hours if you arrive before 10:00 on a weekday), but only if you continue by public transport. When paying at the machine, you scan your OV-chipkaart or GVB P+R ticket. A bank card used to tap in does not count for the discount. Prices as of January 2026.
  • All details: How P+R works (amsterdam.nl)

Returning to the airport

  • Go back the same way: take the train from Amsterdam Muiderpoort to Schiphol.
  • Check the times in the NS app or Google Maps. If the direct connection isn’t running at that time, the app shows you another route.
  • Don’t forget to tap your card again when you leave the tram, metro or station, otherwise you pay the maximum fare.

Shops and the market

The Dappermarkt, supermarkets and bakeries.

The Dappermarkt

  • The street market at the front door has been running since 1910 and is the best place for fruit, vegetables, cheese, fish, nuts and flowers. Open Monday to Saturday.

Supermarkets

  • Albert Heijn Eerste van Swindenstraat 91: around the corner, open every day until late. Regular prices.
  • Lidl Eerste van Swindenstraat 52A: lower prices, with an in-store bakery.
  • About paying by card: many supermarkets and shops in the Netherlands, including Albert Heijn, don’t accept credit cards, and some foreign cards don’t work at all. A debit card (Maestro, V Pay, Visa Debit or Debit Mastercard), or your phone linked to one, is the safest option. It helps to carry a backup card.

Bakeries

  • Olafbrood Dapperstraat 42: sourdough bread, croissants and madeleines. Closed on Sundays.
  • L’Abeille Pastry & Coffee Dapperstraat 7: French pastries and coffee.
  • Baking Lab Linnaeusstraat 99H: bakery and café with pastries, homemade jams and breakfast. Closed on Tuesdays.

International shops

  • Around the Dappermarkt and in the Javastraat you will find many Turkish, Moroccan, Surinamese and Asian shops with products from all over the world.

Going out

Bars, clubs, concerts and theater around the city.

Good to know

  • Party bars are for drinking, dancing and talking. They get busy around 22:00, the music gets louder around midnight, and most are open until 3:00 or 4:00.
  • Clubs are all about dancing. Buy your ticket online in advance. Clubs get busy around midnight and are usually open until 4:00 to 6:00.
  • At the door: bars and clubs in Amsterdam can be strict with big groups, all-male groups and international groups. A small group with someone who speaks Dutch has the best chance. Otherwise, smile at the doorman and keep it short.
  • Getting home: after midnight there are only night buses, so a taxi is often the easiest way back to the apartment.

Leidseplein: Dutch and international crowd, good for bar hopping

  • Café La Bastille, Surprise Bar, Cooldown Café, Bubbles, Café De Gieter, The Waterhole, Chicago Social Club and Kopstootbar.

De Pijp: mostly Dutch crowd

  • Café ’t Paardje and Café Pilsvogel Gerard Douplein, Calle Ocho Albert Cuypstraat and Escobar, plus many other bars in the area for a beer, wine or cocktails.

Clubs

  • Check the club’s website or Instagram for the theme of the night, and know who the main DJ is: the doorman may ask, to see whether you came for the music.

  • Most big clubs in Amsterdam play techno or house. If you like something else, such as reggae, reggaeton or R&B, search online for a party in that style.

Things to do

The Oosterpark, museums and the city center.

In the neighborhood

  • Oosterpark: a large park a few minutes away, nice for a walk or a run.
  • Wereldmuseum Amsterdam Linnaeusstraat 2: museum of world cultures in a beautiful historic building, formerly the Tropenmuseum. About 5 to 10 minutes on foot.
  • Artis Plantage Kerklaan 38-40: Amsterdam’s zoo, about 15 minutes on foot.
  • Verzetsmuseum Dutch Resistance Museum, Plantage Kerklaan 61: daily life in Amsterdam during World War II and the resistance. About 15 minutes on foot.
  • Hortus Botanicus Plantage Middenlaan 2a: one of the oldest botanical gardens in the world, about 20 minutes on foot.

City center

  • Most sights in the center are 20 to 30 minutes away by tram, or 15 to 20 minutes by bike. Google Maps or 9292 shows the best route.
  • Museumplein: Rijksmuseum the famous Dutch paintings of the 17th century, Van Gogh Museum book in advance and Stedelijk Museum modern and contemporary art.
  • Anne Frank House: the house where the Frank family hid during the war. Book tickets online, often weeks in advance.
  • Royal Palace Dam: take the guided tour to learn about Amsterdam in the 17th century.
  • Rembrandt House Museum: the house where Rembrandt lived and worked, about 25 minutes on foot.
  • Museum Van Loon and Our Lord in the Attic: see the inside of 17th-century canal houses. The second has a hidden church in its attic, from a time when other religions were allowed as long as they were not visible from the street.
  • Amsterdam Museum history of the city, FOAM photography and the Heineken Experience the history of Heineken, beer included.
  • NEMO Science Museum and Het Scheepvaartmuseum (National Maritime Museum, with a replica of a 17th-century ship): both by the water, about 25 minutes on foot.
  • Straat Museum: street art and graffiti at the NDSM wharf in Amsterdam-Noord. Take the free ferry from behind Amsterdam Centraal.
  • The complete list of museums: List of museums in Amsterdam (Wikipedia)

Canals

  • Canal cruises leave from many points in the center, for example around Amsterdam Centraal and opposite the Heineken Experience.
  • Nice spots for your Amsterdam canal photo: Spiegelgracht 36, Leidsegracht 68 and Reguliersgracht 88.

Relaxing: sauna

  • Sauna Deco Herengracht: Art Deco sauna in the center.
  • Zuiver Koenenkade: large wellness center with many saunas and pools, next to the Amsterdamse Bos.
  • Volkshotel Wibautstraat: rooftop hot tubs and sauna, about 15 minutes away.
